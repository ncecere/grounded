# v0.10.0: private content

Status: **planned, 2026-11-05. The decisions below are recommendations awaiting the owner.** Scope comes from the
release plan ([`roadmap.md`](roadmap.md) § L, owner 2026-10-04): document permissions first, then the Microsoft 365
connector, then Google Drive and the other connectors. The detailed design for document permissions is in
[`specs/document-acls/DESIGN.md`](specs/document-acls/DESIGN.md).

## Scope

| # | Item | Roadmap | Issue |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Document permissions**: a document can be restricted to people and groups; search, answers, citations and every other place that shows its text apply the asker's permissions, failing closed | B6 | #41 |
| 2 | **Microsoft 365 connector**: SharePoint sites and OneDrive, with delta sync, deletions, and each file's permissions mapped to (1) | B1 | #42 |
| 3 | **Google Drive connector**: My Drive and shared drives, with change tokens and permissions | B2 | #43 |
| 4 | **Other connectors**, each in its own increment: Confluence, Canvas LMS, Box, GitHub, S3 | B3 | #44 |

(1) comes first: no connector that reads private content ships before Grounded can enforce who may see it.

## Decisions to confirm

Each has a recommendation, with the reasons in the design.

1. **What a restriction is.** A document is either unrestricted, as every document is today, or restricted to a list
   of principals. A restricted document with an empty list is visible to nobody. **Recommended.**
2. **Who a principal is.** Matching uses:
   - a Grounded person;
   - the identity provider's subject or object ID (the same ID SCIM matches);
   - a **verified** email address;
   - an identity-provider group, as sent at sign-in or by SCIM;
   - a connector's own groups, expanded to their members by the connector.

   Email is used here, though never for linking accounts, because Google Drive and Box share by email. An unverified
   email never matches. **Recommended.**
3. **Who can ever see restricted content: only a person.** That means a signed-in person in the app, or the person's
   own connected app on MCP. **Never** an API key, a publishable key, the public page or widget, a webhook, or an
   evaluation run, even when a person owns the key. **Recommended:** a key is a program, not someone whose access
   Grounded can check.
4. **Editors and admins.** They see a restricted document's name, status and who it's restricted to, which they need
   in order to manage it. They see its **text** only if they are on its list, like anyone else. Platform admins reach
   the text through break-glass, which is audited and exists for this. **Recommended.**
5. **Answer cache and sharing.**
   - An answer that used a restricted passage is never cached.
   - An agent whose knowledge contains restricted documents serves no cached answers, because a cached answer would be
     another person's retrieval.
   - A conversation whose answers cited a restricted passage can't be shared. A person who later loses access keeps
     their own conversation, but its restricted citations no longer open.

   **Recommended.**
6. **Restricting by hand.** Editors can restrict a whole source (uploads or web) to groups or people, which is useful
   on its own, for example an HR folder. Per-document permissions come from connectors and the API.
   **Recommended:** this also lets B6 be tested before a connector exists.
7. **How fast a change applies.**
   - Permissions are checked when the question is asked, never cached.
   - A document's list changes at the connector's next sync, or at once when set by hand.
   - A person's groups change at their next sign-in (from the groups claim), or at once through SCIM.
   - Removing a person, or a SCIM suspension, applies at once.

   **Recommended.**
8. **Connectors' credentials and tenants.** Each connector is built against its provider's documented API, with
   recorded test fixtures. Real tenants are used only with the owner's credentials and permission: a Microsoft 365
   developer tenant and a Google Workspace test domain. **The owner needs to provide or approve these.**

Document permissions are behind a platform switch, off by default and turned on after the upgrade's rollout: a pod of
the previous release would ignore a restriction.

## Not in v0.10.0

- Crawling sites that need a login (B5; it builds on (1) and comes after).
- Permission-aware analytics (the gap report and analytics keep counting questions without document text).
